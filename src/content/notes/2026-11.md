This is the first edition of the field notes. Each month they answer one question: **what changed in the field that should change how you understand or use these systems?** Most months, the honest answer will be "less than the headlines suggest".

## Decision models

Since September, a kind of model that doesn't write text at all has taken off. TypeSafe AI released Jev on 15 September, and open alternatives followed within weeks: Laya, AWS's Strands Decider and Cloudflare's Clef. All of them take a situation plus a narrow question with fixed answers ("allow, ask a human, or block?") and return a probability for every option in a single pass, in tens to hundreds of milliseconds.

Under the hood this is less exotic than it sounds. Strands Decider, for example, is an ordinary pretrained language model whose last layer, the one that scores every possible next word, has been replaced by a small one that scores only the options you pass in.

Agent systems are full of small yes/no and pick-one decisions: is this input suspicious, may this tool run, is the task finished? Each one used to mean a full call to a big model or a custom classifier. Decision models make those checkpoints cheap enough to put everywhere. The new chapter [Around the model](../../learn/around-the-model/) has a live demo of one acting as a safety gate.

Stay sceptical, though. The makers' benchmark numbers are self-reported. Independent tests came fast, mostly of Jev, and their quality is uneven: an audit of the first 28 papers found that most lacked a like-for-like baseline, and that the typed answer format itself had shown no accuracy advantage, only gains in speed and cost. One study found Jev answering "cheap" for 99.5% of model-routing requests, which is no better than always giving the same answer. A fast probability is only useful if it's calibrated, so measure on your own decisions before trusting one.

## The patch wave

From July on, the number of published vulnerabilities jumped. In the public NVD and CISA data, critical CVEs published from August to October came to about three times the figure for the same months of 2025, while vulnerabilities newly confirmed as exploited rose much less, by under double. Vendors put the surge down to AI-assisted bug finding: Microsoft's October update alone fixed 1,413 flaws, more than in all of 2025. Finding flaws got cheap and fixing them didn't, so the bottleneck for security teams is now patching. The new chapter [The patch gap](../../learn/patch-gap/) charts the data month by month and refreshes it with each update.

## The first frontier radar

The [radar](../../radar/) now tracks 22 directions across architecture, training, systems and security. This month's placements are the baseline. From next month on, you'll be able to replay how entries move. Two placements worth explaining:

- **Diffusion language models: emerging.** A survey reports several-fold speed-ups at comparable quality, but a newer study found they currently hallucinate more than comparable autoregressive models.
- **Prompt-injection defences: shipping, not solved.** Classifiers and instruction-hierarchy training are in mainstream products, so by the radar's own definition they are shipping. None of them solves the underlying problem that instructions and data share one channel.

## What we're watching

- **Test-time training** (models that learn while they read): two strong papers in the past year. It's the direction most likely to change the "two memories" picture in this guide.
- **Whether decision models hold up** under independent evaluation, and whether open rebuilds close the gap to Jev.
- **Who approves an agent's actions.** Since August, Claude Code lets a classifier approve most actions instead of asking the user. Anthropic's own study explains why: people in it stopped few of the dangerous commands they were shown. A researcher got past the classifier the same month. A boundary the agent can't cross still beats a reviewer of any kind.
- **Prompt injection, measured.** OWASP's new list of the biggest risks in LLM applications keeps prompt injection in first place and moves excessive agency, an agent with more access than its job needs, from sixth to third. The labs' own attack-success numbers are now small, and one of them got worse this month.
- **Agent time horizons.** METR's 20 November update says the task length frontier agents finish half the time now doubles about every 3.6 months, and also warns that its own tests may overestimate real-world ability.
