Most research directions aim at one of the limits this guide has described. Grouped by the problem they try to solve:

**"It can't learn after training."** *Test-time training* lets a model write what it reads into a small, changeable part of itself while it works, so knowledge isn't stuck between frozen weights and a temporary conversation. <Claim id="ttt-inplace" cap />, and <Claim id="ttt-e2e" />. It is still research, aimed at the gap between the two memories.

**"Long texts are slow and expensive."** *Hybrid models* replace most attention layers with layers that keep a fixed-size running summary, so they're cheaper on long texts but can forget exact details; a few full-attention layers stay for exact recall. These are already in some open models, and <Claim id="subquadratic-review" />.

**"Writing one token at a time is slow."** *Diffusion language models* start with a blank draft and fill in many positions at once, refining over several passes, the way image generators work. <Claim id="dlm-status" cap />, though <Claim id="dlm-hallucination" />.

<Diffusion />

**"Thinking in words is inefficient."** *Latent reasoning* lets a model think in its internal numbers instead of writing out each step, or loop its own layers to think longer. That is more efficient, but it gives up the readable chain of thought. The written chain is one of the few views into what a model is doing, though an imperfect one: models don't always write down what drove their answer. Looped layers have now left the lab: <Claim id="glm-loop" />. And the view is already narrowing in a model released to the public: <Claim id="astra-monitorability" />. OpenAI doesn't say how that model is built, so the drop can't be pinned on any one design.

**"Language isn't the world."** *World models* learn how situations unfold, especially physical ones, by predicting in an abstract space rather than word by word. <Claim id="ami-labs" cap />; <Claim id="ami-product" />.

**"Research itself is slow."** Agents are starting to run AI experiments themselves, on small models for now. <Claim id="autoresearch" cap />.

## Reading the frontier sceptically

<Note title="What has driven progress" kind="key">
Most of the progress you've felt in the last two years came not from new architectures but from better training (especially practice with automatic checking) and from letting models think longer, on the 2017 design (with many refinements, but the same core idea). "Transformers are about to be replaced" has been predicted every year since 2023. The likely near-term path is absorption: hybrid layers, test-time memory and diffusion-style decoding get folded into transformer-like systems rather than replacing them. The two bets that would change the picture most, continual learning and world models, are also the least proven.
</Note>

<Debate id="imitation-dead-end" />

<Note title="Security lens" kind="warn">
Better reasoning about code is also better reasoning about flaws in code. <Claim id="msrc-ai" cap />. The result is more vulnerabilities found than anyone can fix, a shift that [The patch gap](/learn/patch-gap/) in Part 3 follows month by month.
</Note>

<Engineer title="Which limitation each direction targets">
<FrontierMatrix />

● main target, ○ side benefit.

**Cheaper long context.** <Term k="ssm">SSMs</Term> such as Mamba ([Gu and Dao, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752)) and <Term k="linear">linear attention</Term> such as Gated DeltaNet keep a fixed-size state instead of a growing cache, so their memory doesn't grow with the length of the text, but their recall is lossy. Hybrids place a few full-attention layers between them to keep exact recall.

**Parallel writing.** <Term k="dlm">Diffusion LMs</Term> come in masked (discrete) and continuous variants. A discrete version competitive with GPT-2 won an ICML 2024 best paper ([Lou, Meng and Ermon, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16834)), and Inception Labs launched Mercury as the first commercial-scale one ([Inception Labs, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17298)). Each pass costs more than one autoregressive step, but it fills in many positions at once.

**Learning while reading.** <Term k="ttt">TTT</Term> updates a set of fast-changing weights during inference, using a self-supervised objective on the text being read.

**Thinking without words.** <Term k="latent">Latent reasoning</Term> includes continuous chain-of-thought (Meta's Coconut) and recurrent-depth models that loop their own layers. Tiny recursive models trained only on puzzles reach 45% on <Term k="arc">ARC-AGI</Term>-1 and 8% on the harder ARC-AGI-2, though they aren't general language models.

**World models.** <Term k="jepa">JEPA</Term>-style models predict embeddings, compact summaries of what comes next, rather than pixels or tokens.

**Where things stand.** <Claim id="transformer-dominance" cap />.
</Engineer>
