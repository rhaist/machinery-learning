When you use an AI product, you're rarely talking to one model. Behind the scenes there is usually a small team, each member doing what it's best and cheapest at:

<Note title="Like a hospital">
The **senior doctor** (a large frontier model) handles the hard, open-ended cases. A **triage nurse** (a decision model) makes quick, narrow calls: urgent or not, which department. **Lab technicians** (small models) run routine jobs fast and cheaply. And the **hospital's procedures** (plain software, the harness) decide who is allowed to do what, and keep records.
</Note>

- **Large frontier models** do the open-ended thinking: understanding the task, planning, writing code.
- **<Term k="slm">Small language models</Term>** are the same kind of model made much smaller. They're fast, cheap, private, and can run on a laptop or phone, but they know less and are weaker at long reasoning. They're good for summarising, sorting and extracting.
- **<Term k="decision">Decision models</Term>** are new in autumn 2026. They don't write text at all. You ask a narrow question with fixed answers ("allow, ask a human, or block?") and get back a probability for each option, in about a tenth of a second. <Claim id="jev-release" />, and AWS followed with an open version.
- **The <Term k="harness">harness</Term>** is ordinary software: the loop that passes messages, runs tools, enforces permissions and keeps the conversation. Agent frameworks such as the Strands Agents SDK live here. (The Strands project also released a decision model, Strands Decider, which is why the names blur.)

## Try it: a safety gate for an AI's actions

An AI agent proposes a command. A decision model scores how risky it is, and plain, auditable code applies the rule. The model supplies a judgement; the policy stays in code you can read.

<Note title="Measure before you trust it" kind="warn">
A fast, confident number is not the same as a correct one. <Claim id="decision-imitation" />, and <Claim id="jev-routing" />. A gate built on a decision model is still probabilistic.
</Note>

<Engineer title="Decision models as a category">
A decision model keeps the entire pretrained transformer and swaps only the unembedding layer from the stack chapter. Instead of scoring ~200k vocabulary tokens, a small pointer head scores *your* options in a single forward pass. The torso keeps everything it learned about language; the model just loses the ability to ramble. Question types are typically predicate (yes/no), choice (one of N) and score.

**Benchmarks in a two-month-old category:** <Claim id="decider-bench" />. Numbers depend on who ran them; measure on your own decisions.

**Harnesses and MCP.** <Claim id="strands-sdk" />. Harnesses run tools, manage context and retries, and increasingly connect to tool servers over <Term k="mcp">MCP</Term>. The common deployment pattern is a <Term k="cascade">cascade</Term>: a cheap model or decision model handles everything, acts on confident answers, and escalates the uncertain tail.

**SLMs in practice.** Usually 0.5–15B parameters, made capable through <Term k="distill">distillation</Term> and runnable locally through <Term k="quant">quantisation</Term>. Typical roles: summarising a 50k-token log before it reaches the expensive model, extraction, classification, on-device features with data that must not leave the machine.
</Engineer>
