# Expert review

Automated fact-checks catch a lot, but they share blind spots with the model that drafted the text. A person who works in the field catches what they miss. This page is the brief for a reviewer: what to read, what to look for, and how to send findings back.

## Who and what

| Reviewer | Reads | Time |
|---|---|---|
| ML engineer or researcher | Part 1, chapters 2–10: the Engineer boxes first, then the main text | 60–90 minutes |
| Security practitioner | Part 2, all five chapters, including the labs and Engineer boxes | 60 minutes |
| Anyone new to the topic | Part 1, chapter 1 and one chapter of their choice, Engineer boxes closed | 20 minutes |

Read on the live site: <https://machinerylearning.eu>. To open every Engineer box at once, choose Menu › Settings › Engineer boxes › Open.

## What to look for

1. **Wrong or outdated facts.** Numbers, dates, names, who did what. Each time-sensitive fact has a date stamp; click it to see the source.
2. **Explanations an expert would object to.** A simplification is fine if it doesn't teach something false. "Not quite how it works" is exactly the feedback we need.
3. **Figures.** Do the drawings and step-throughs show the mechanism correctly? Are the sizes right for the model they name?
4. **Missing caveats.** Places where the guide sounds more certain than the evidence allows, or where a well-known counter-argument is missing.
5. **For newcomers:** the first sentence you had to read twice, and the first term that wasn't explained.

Style, wording and layout feedback is welcome but secondary.

## How to report

- Quickest: the "Report it on GitHub" link at the end of each chapter, or the "Report" link next to each fact on the [sources page](https://machinerylearning.eu/sources/). Both open a prefilled issue.
- Or send one list by email to hello@machinerylearning.eu: page, quote, problem, and a source if you have one.

## What happens next

Every confirmed error is fixed and listed on the [corrections page](https://machinerylearning.eu/corrections/) with what was wrong and how it was found. Reviewers are thanked by name on the About page if they want to be.
